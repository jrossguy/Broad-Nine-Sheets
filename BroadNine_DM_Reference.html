The Pact System — Cosmological Framework

DM Eyes Only — Players experience pacts through their effects, not their origin structure.

The Eternal War

Between the planes of Heaven and Hell, a war has been fought since before recorded history. The combatants are entities of extraordinary power — beings who developed along angelic or demonic lines and dedicate their existence to the conflict's outcome. Most never reach a definitive resolution. Some do.

At Level 30, a combatant on either side becomes too powerful for the war to contain. An entity at this tier represents a force that, if unleashed freely in the eternal conflict, would cause damage neither side can absorb. By agreement, tradition, or necessity — the accounts differ — combatants who reach Level 30 are removed from the war and relocated permanently.

This is not imprisonment. It is retirement under constraint.

The White Reach (Angelic Residence — Earth 3)

The highest point on Earth 3. Above the cloud line. Above weather. The sky at this elevation behaves differently — light bends, temperatures hold absolute still, and the architecture that has grown there over millennia belongs to no known human tradition.

The celestial entities who reside here are not imprisoned. They chose to come to a place appropriate to what they are. They watch the war continue below them. They watch Earth 9 and the other Earths with varying degrees of investment.

Known residents: - Ra — Solar entity. Aligned with judgment, light, and cosmic order. Among the most interventionist of the celestial residents — has never been comfortable watching from a distance. Active pact network. - Xuanwu (玄武) — The Black Tortoise of the North. Patient watcher. Chose one pact in recent history (Shin). May be the most selective of all residents — this was not his first opportunity to contract and may be his last. - Queen of Stars — Left the Reach briefly to establish a pact. Identity of the pact holder not publicly known. - Others TBD as the campaign develops.

The Dark Pit (Infernal Residence — Earth 3)

The deepest point on Earth 3. Below the ocean floor. Below geological activity. A place that exists in a silence so complete that even the infernal entities who reside there find it striking after the constant noise of the eternal war.

They are no less active than the celestial residents. The absence of sound does not mean the absence of purpose.

Known residents: - Sparda — Left the Pit briefly to establish a contract with a mortal. Among the most active of the infernal residents in terms of direct intervention. The decision to contract was deliberate and the pact holder is significant. - Others TBD as the campaign develops.

Rules of Residence

A resident of either location may only leave for two reasons:

1. Combat with another Level 30 entity. If a resident chooses to fight — another L30 entity, a Chosen Mortal, or another power of comparable magnitude — they may leave for the duration of that engagement. Most never exercise this. The war produced too many losses already.

2. Establishing a contract. A resident may leave the Reach or the Pit briefly to find a worthy mortal and offer a pact. The mortal must be in a location accessible to the entity. The contract itself takes no more than a moment if the mortal accepts. The entity returns immediately after. This mechanism — indirect influence through pact holders — has become the primary way both sides continue to prosecute the war without technically violating the terms of removal.

"Heavily favored through history" is accurate. Most residents have established at least one pact. Some maintain several simultaneously.

Why Mortals Accept

A pact with a Level 30 entity is the most significant power augmentation available in the system. The entity's capabilities channel through the holder during active channeling. The Chr investment required is real — the relationship must be cultivated — but the ceiling is absolute.

Most pact holders do not know they are proxies in a war between Heaven and Hell. They know they have an entity. They know the power is real. The broader context — the eternal war, the residences, the deliberate strategic placement of influence in the mortal world — is not disclosed.

Some have figured it out. None have been able to do anything about it.

Xuanwu and Shin — Special Case

Xuanwu did not wait for Shin to seek him out. He left the White Reach to find an infant — a Chinese child of extraordinary latent potential placed into an environment that would systematically suppress what made him remarkable. The Pact was not offered in exchange for service. It was a watch. Xuanwu chose to protect Shin until Shin could protect himself.

Whether this was strategic — a piece placed deliberately for later use — or genuine affection from a deity who has watched too many potential lives be wasted by circumstance, even the other residents of the White Reach cannot say. Xuanwu does not discuss it.

When Shin's Nen surfaces and he arrives home, the pact will complete. Xuanwu will step back. What Shin does with the capabilities he's developed — including eventually combining his Fylgja and his Nen, something no mortal has done — will play out without a celestial safety net.

Whether Xuanwu is watching when it happens is a different question.

Shin (United Knights Defector — Chinese Origin — Level 20)

Status: Independent. Traveling toward China. Currently in Chinese border territories. Level: 20 · Legendary Tier (2d20+d6) · Nat 20 Threshold: 39+ Appearance: Image ref: Shin.png — ornate gold-and-dark armor, blindfolded, white-grey hair, wide smile that reads as unconcerned with what is in front of him. Age: Comparable in training to Derek VI — adopted in infancy, raised from childhood alongside him.

Background

Shin was found as an infant and brought to the United Knights — the exact circumstances are unknown to everyone still living except possibly the Emperor of China's inner circle. He was adopted young enough that the Knights were his entire world. He was raised alongside Derek VI. He trained alongside Derek. When Derek became the heir apparent, Shin was the shadow behind the throne that the inner circle trusted and the public barely knew existed.

He was, by all internal accounts, Derek's closest companion. Not a bodyguard. Not an advisor. Something more personal than either.

He earned his Fylgja at Aldricslond. Survived both trials. Forged his soul-bound pauldrons and gauntlets in the volcano's lava. The form that emerged — armor pieces rather than a weapon — was read by the Knights as appropriate for someone who fought with his body. The Fylgja augments and is his hands and shoulders. It is the most personal expression of his soul that he ever allowed himself.

He also holds a Pact with Xuanwu, though he has never fully understood why a celestial entity chose him specifically. He accepted the Pact at a point of personal uncertainty and found it stabilizing. He has never pushed to understand it further.

He has always had an affinity for Nen. He never touched it. The Knights legislate against it. He was a Knight to his core. He chose not to inquire.

The Defection

When Shin learned the truth of his origin — that he was Chinese, that he had been removed from China as an infant for reasons that remain unclear, that his entire Knight identity was built on a foundation that wasn't native to him — he did not renounce the Knights aggressively. He did not announce a defection. He left.

This was, in Derek's experience, worse than an announcement would have been.

Derek has not pursued him. This restraint costs Derek something real. The inner circle knows this. Nobody has suggested that the restraint is weakness.

What the Knights know: Shin has left. His Fylgja — technically a soul-bound weapon forged under Knight covenant — went with him. This is treated as an institutional matter, not merely personal. A Fylgja leaving Knight hands, held by someone who no longer pledges loyalty, is not a small thing.

What Derek actually feels about it: Between him and the wall he stares at when the room is empty.

Current State

He is traveling through Chinese territories. He does not speak the language yet beyond fragments. He does not know anyone. He is, for the first time in his life, oriented toward something rather than standing behind someone else's orientation.

He is also beginning to feel his Nen. He does not have a name for what it is. He has no tradition, no teacher, no framework. He just knows that something is happening that the Knights always told him was wrong, and that it doesn't feel wrong at all. It feels like arriving somewhere he has been before.

His Pact with Xuanwu has been quieter than usual since he crossed into Chinese territory. He does not know if this is significant.

Combat Profile

Fighting style: Redirect and counter. Based on the principle that force is not destroyed — it is redirected. Every attack directed at Shin is a gift. He accepts it, changes its direction, and returns it.

He does not strike first. He does not chase. He occupies space and waits for opponents to commit. When they do, they discover that what they committed to is no longer there — and what is there instead is a counter-strike carrying their own momentum back at them.

This style requires extraordinary patience and superior defensive mechanics. Shin has both.

Unarmed: His Fylgja gauntlets are his weapons. Grade 4 weapon die (1d10) amplified by his Grade 5 armor's properties. He strikes rarely and decisively.

The Fylgja — Pauldrons and Gauntlets

Soul-bound pauldrons and gauntlets forged at Aldricslond. The form emerged from Shin's soul during the forging — the Knights interpreted this as a martial soul that expressed itself through the body rather than through an external weapon. They were not wrong about that interpretation. They did not understand its full implications.

Fylgja ↔ Nen — Fundamental Opposition: Both systems use the soul as their foundational development tool. The Fylgja is the soul expressed outward as a permanent weapon. Nen is the soul turned inward as cultivatable energy. They pull the soul in opposite directions simultaneously — the soul cannot be both the forge and the furnace.

Using both at once has never been done. Shin will eventually become the first person in history to do so. This is not a mechanical achievement. It is a statement about who he is — that his soul is large enough to contain both expressions without choosing between them.

When Shin defected, the Fylgja went with him. This is treated by the Knights as an institutional violation as much as a personal one. A Bankai that leaves Knight hands is a weapon without its covenant.

The Pact — Xuanwu (玄武)

Entity: Xuanwu — the Black Tortoise of the North. Celestial resident of the White Reach on Earth 3. Left his residence briefly to contract an infant of extraordinary potential before that potential could be fully suppressed.

Chr Training Tier 5 — developed through a lifetime of the relationship existing, not through deliberate investment. Shin did not pursue this pact. It was given to him.

Covenant Favor: 60 (Chr +4 × 10, + T5 bonus 20) Covenant Depth: Minor Drain while channeling: Moderate (−10/turn)

When channeling Xuanwu: His Block becomes extraordinary — Xuanwu's defensive nature amplifies Shin's own. Physical transformation at T4: skin takes on a faintly scaled quality, eyes shift to deep black, posture becomes preternaturally still. The tortoise and serpent manifest through him without literal transformation.

The Pact's End: When Shin fully arrives in China and his Nen surfaces, Xuanwu will complete his watch. The pact will end not through renunciation but through fulfillment — Xuanwu's purpose was to protect Shin until Shin could protect himself. The protection is no longer needed. The Pact completes. The Chr investment Shin made does not become meaningless — it was the cost of being kept safe until now.

What Shin does after that without a safety net is his alone.

The Emperor Connection

Shin has loose connections to the Emperor of China's inner circle — the nature and source of these connections are unknown to Shin himself. He does not know that he was taken from China deliberately. He does not know what significance his origin carries.

The Emperor is a Brain in a Vat — an immortal of extraordinary capability who operates through remote systems. Whether the Emperor knows about Shin's existence, origin, and approach is a question for when Shin actually arrives.

Relationship to Derek VI

Derek adopted Shin young enough that the distinction between brother and friend never fully formed — Shin was just part of what Derek was. The loss of Shin has been processed by Derek as something between betrayal and bereavement. He has not decided which.

He has not sent anyone after Shin. He has not declared him an enemy. He has not revoked the Fylgja covenant — even if that were possible, he hasn't tried. He is doing what Derek does with things he doesn't know how to process: continuing to function.

Chosen Mortality System

Applies to any Level 30 character who formally renounces immortality — a deliberate narrative decision requiring DM approval. There will be more than one.

A character who chooses mortality at Level 30 is making a statement: this life, this finite stretch, matters more to me than endless continuation. They are burning deliberately. Every session that passes is a session they chose not to have forever.

PASSIVE — Fearless

Cannot be Frightened, Intimidated, or have morale-breaking status effects applied. Ever. Psychological pressure has no purchase on someone who has already decided to die. This includes: - Combat Intimidate (Chr T2) - Enemy Presence aura effects that cause fear - Status effects: Frightened, Demoralized, Broken - Any ability that functions by threatening the target's survival

Immortals fear losing their endless life. Chosen Mortals do not.

ACTIVE — Mortality Surge (once per round)

Spend 20 permanent max HP — not current HP, max HP, non-recoverable. The next roll made this round automatically achieves a Nat 20 result regardless of dice. No roll required. The character has decided what this moment means and their body delivers it.

Rules: - Declared before the roll - Applies to any roll — attack, defense, skill check, save - The HP reduction is permanent and cannot be restored by any means - A character who burns enough HP through Mortality Surges will eventually die from it — which is the point

Ara (Soul in Conditio Ultima)

Status: Dead — soul bound in Conditio Ultima, the six custom batarangs held by Bruce Wayne. Build: Force practitioner. Immortal in life. One of the older practitioners of the tradition before it became formally associated with the Preserve. Name origin: From the Southern Cross constellation — meaning altar. No strong loyalty to them and no strong hatred — only clarity that the pattern was going to end something she cared about. When the Guild made their case during the Earth 8 transition, she listened. When they finished: "You're right about one thing. Someone has to be here when the next one comes."

She proposed the weapon herself. The Batman of that era did not ask. She spent three weeks deciding on the form — six batarangs, not one weapon. She wanted the lineage to have flexibility. She wanted to leave something that could be used carefully.

"Don't waste them. Save one. You'll know why when you see it."

History: During the Earth 8 Syzygy transition the Great Families were moving to claim Earth 8 as their next destination. The weapon was forged as a peace negotiation artifact: the Guild and the Batman of the era worked together, Ara volunteered, and the forging happened at the apex of the Syzygy transition itself — when universal Nen moved through everything. The universal Nen that attached to the batarangs was not invited and cannot be replicated.

The weapon: Grade 6 to Bruce Wayne and Robin only. To anyone else — the soul withdraws completely. Grade 1. No aggression. Complete indifference. She agreed to serve the lineage, not the world.

Cannot communicate with wielders. Her choice. A weapon that whispered advice would become a crutch. She decided silence was the right answer and has kept it for 1000 years.

Network presence: The most attentive presence in the Grade 6 Soul Network. Every soul knows she is listening. None know what she has concluded. Not dormant — Tiānjī waits for a moment. Ara decided silence was correct and has never changed her mind. The network has learned not to interpret her silence as absence.

The Syzygy batarang: Bruce has six. Five are conventional Grade 6 use — devastating, finite. One is different. Neither Bruce nor anyone alive knows which one, or what the universal Nen inside it does when the Syzygy conditions recur. The answer died with the Batman who worked with Ara. The answer is in the weapon, in the network, and in whatever records survived the thousand years between then and now.

Shin (United Knights Defector — En Route to China)

Level: 20 · Tier: Legendary (2d20+d6) · Nat 20: 39+ Appearance: Image ref — Shin.png (blindfolded, ornate gold and purple armor, striking grin) Status: Independent · Currently in Chinese territories, exploring origins Relationship to Derek: Adopted brother. Heavy personal blow to Derek upon departure. Known to the inner circle and senior Knights. Not public beyond that.

Who He Is

Shin was brought into the United Knights' royal family as an infant. Nobody in the family knew his origin at the time — Derek included. He was raised alongside Derek as a brother, trained in the same traditions, forged in the same fires. His entire identity was Knight-born. Everything about him — his fighting style, his values, his Fylgja — came from that upbringing.

He was always different in ways nobody examined too closely. The latent Nen affinity he never developed manifested as an unusual intuition, a physical sensitivity nobody else had. He didn't understand what it was. He never pursued it. The Knights legislated against Nen. He was a Knight. That was the end of that line of thought.

When he learned his true origin — Chinese, with loose bloodline connections to the Emperor — the ground shifted. Not violently. Quietly, completely, and without any clear resolution. He wasn't angry. He wasn't betrayed. He was simply a person who had been told a story about himself his entire life and discovered the story was incomplete.

He left. That was the hardest part — not the leaving, but what he left. Derek. The forge. The Fylgja that was his soul made physical.

Fighting Style — Redirect Flow

Based on the internal martial arts tradition that traces its philosophy to Xuanwu: yield to force, redirect it, punish commitment. Shin does not block attacks. He receives them — through the shoulder, through the armor, through his body — and returns them through his fists, amplified.

Attacking Shin is a mistake. The harder the strike, the harder it comes back. His unarmed strikes hit like weapons because the armor makes them so. His Block is not resistance — it is redirection. When he counters, he doesn't strike from his own power. He gives the attacker's power back to them.

He fights standing still. He does not chase. The redirect style only works if the opponent commits, and opponents only commit if they believe he's not dangerous. Most believe this, once.

Fylgja — The Ironhold (Pauldrons and Gauntlets)

Forged at Aldricseld. The Bankai took the form of armored pauldrons and gauntlets — shoulder guards and fists. An appropriate expression: the shoulders absorb, the fists return.

When active, the Ironhold enhances both his Block and his counter strikes. The pauldrons carry the force of incoming blows. The gauntlets deliver them back.

The complication: Holding a Fylgja requires complete loyalty to the United Knights. Shin defected. He still carries the Ironhold. This is not technically possible within Knight law — and yet the Bankai was forged from his soul, not from his politics. The Fylgja does not know he left. The Knights do. Derek does.

Derek has not moved to reclaim or void the Fylgja. Whether this is inability or unwillingness has not been established.

The Nen conflict: The Ironhold and Shin's latent Nen affinity are fundamentally opposed — see Fylgja/Nen Opposition note. He cannot use both simultaneously. Currently he doesn't use either. The Nen is untouched. The Fylgja is carried, not activated. He is a man walking around with two loaded weapons he refuses to fire because he doesn't yet understand which one he is.

Grade 5 Custom Armor — The Vessel

Custom-forged to complement both his unarmed fighting style and the Ironhold Fylgja. The armor covers everything the Fylgja doesn't — torso, legs, back. Together they form a complete shell.

Armor HP: ~170 (reads as one grade higher via Con T1) Movement bonus: +5 beyond training cap

Special Property 1 — Reflected Force: When Shin successfully Blocks an attack with a margin of 5+, the counter is not a die roll. The attacker's full damage roll is returned to them as a direct counter strike. He doesn't hit back. He returns what was sent.

Special Property 2 — Unarmed Amplification: Unarmed strikes deal damage as if using a Grade 4 weapon die (1d10, upgraded to 1d12 via Str T3). The armor's construction distributes force through the body such that a bare palm delivers like forged steel.

Int 30+ required to repair. No one in the field has that. If it breaks, it stays broken until a qualified smith can be found.

The Xuanwu Pact

Entity: Xuanwu (玄武) — The Black Tortoise · Angel · White Reach resident Depth: Minor (Chr 16, 15+ threshold) Favor Pool: 180 (Chr 16 × 10 + T5 +20) Passive Gain: +3 Favor/turn outside channel (Legendary tier) Drain while channeling: −5/turn (Minor entity)

Xuanwu's attribute bonus while channeling: Constitution — flat HP buffer and enhanced Block roll.

Shin has been in a pact with Xuanwu since childhood. He didn't seek it. Xuanwu found him — a Chinese child of extraordinary latent potential, placed in an environment that would suppress exactly what made him remarkable. The pact's purpose was protective: keep him alive and guided until he could arrive somewhere he could understand himself.

Xuanwu has never mentioned this explicitly. He has offered protection and steady counsel in the way an entity who has been watching over someone for decades offers counsel — without drama, without urgency, without ever quite saying what he's waiting for.

When Shin's Nen surfaces in China, Xuanwu will step back. Not through conflict. Through completion.

DM note: The pact did not end when Shin left the Knights. Xuanwu is not a Knight institution. The covenant is personal. The one question that has not been asked: did Xuanwu know about Shin's Chinese origin all along? The answer is yes. Whether Shin asks is up to the session.

Current Situation

Shin is somewhere in Chinese territory. He is not being hunted — the Knights have not formally issued a bounty. Derek has not authorized action against him. The departure is raw and recent and Derek has not decided what to do with it yet.

Shin is exploring his origins. Not urgently. He doesn't have a destination. He has a direction — toward understanding who he actually is, stripped of the identity he was given. The Nen affinity he's been suppressing for twenty years is beginning to surface now that the structure around it has been removed. He can feel it. He doesn't know what it is yet.

He carries the Ironhold. He doesn't activate it. He carries the Vessel. He doesn't use the Pact aggressively. He is a man between things — not yet what he will become, and no longer what he was.

Session role: Will be encountered in Chinese territories during Project 66 or the China arc. He is not hostile to players. He is also not a friendly — he doesn't know who they are, and someone finding him in China while he's figuring out what he is will be approached with cautious assessment. The right approach reaches him. The wrong one reveals how dangerous the redirect style is in practice.

Key Relationships

Derek: Brother — adopted, raised together, trained together. The departure was the heaviest thing Derek has experienced personally in recent memory. He has not spoken of it publicly. He hasn't decided what it means yet.

The Emperor (Brain): Loose bloodline connection. Shin doesn't know the nature of this. The Emperor may know Shin exists. Whether they've ever been in contact is TBD.

Xuanwu: Decades-long pact. More personal than Shin understands. Xuanwu has been patient for a very long time.

Sion: Derek's son. Also secretly harbors Nen. They know each other from the family circle. Whether they know about each other's Nen is TBD.

Nadia (Soul in Prima Conscientia)

No power system. No training. Just Nadia. How she became Level 30: Steiner's creature-fusion experiment. Merged with a creature possessing laser beam capability. Fylgja and Nen — The Soul Opposition

(DM Eyes Only — New System Rule)

Fundamental rule: A character cannot actively use both a Fylgja (Bankai) and an active Nen Ability in the same combat. They are not mechanically opposed — they are ontologically opposed.

Both systems use the soul as their foundational development tool.

Fylgja is the soul expressed outward as a bound weapon — a physical manifestation of the wielder's deepest self. Forging a Bankai is an act of complete self-revelation. The weapon IS the person. Using it requires the soul to be singular, coherent, and fully invested in that one expression.

Nen is the soul turned inward as energy, then shaped and projected. Developing a Nen Ability requires the soul to be turned on itself — examined, understood, and developed from within.

The soul cannot be both at once. It cannot be the forge and the furnace simultaneously. Attempting to use both in the same moment is not painful or dangerous — it is simply impossible. One cancels the other. The practitioner must choose which expression they are using at any given moment.

His journey to China is the process of containing both truths simultaneously rather than choosing between them. The achievement is not mechanical. It is a statement about who he is: a soul complete enough to be two opposed things at once without breaking.

GRADE 6 ARMOR — SYSTEM

Grade 6 armor follows the same soul logic as Grade 6 weapons — a Level 30 soul inside, voluntarily or otherwise. The key differences:

Not invincible — erosive. Grade 6 armor does not make the wearer immune to damage indefinitely. Each hit absorbed erodes the soul's capacity. Most armors can absorb significant punishment before the soul is depleted — but they are finite, and each hit costs something real.

Soul Pool — erosion rates: - Hits from Grade 1–3 weapons or low-tier opponents: −1 per hit - Hits from Grade 4 weapons: −2 per hit - Hits from Grade 5 weapons: −3 to −5 per hit (severity determines cost) - Hits from Grade 6 weapons or Level 30 opponents: −6 to −10 per hit - Critical hits: +1 additional on top of base cost

When the Soul Pool reaches 0, the soul is depleted and the armor becomes inert. The soul is not destroyed in all cases — some may dissipate into the Grade 6 Soul Network. Others simply cease. The DM determines the outcome based on the soul's nature.

Unrepairable. Engineering rolls, Repair Kits, and smithing cannot restore Soul Pool. What is spent is spent.

Grade 1–5 armor follows the standard system. Int 20+ required for Grade 4 repair. Int 30+ required for Grade 5 repair. Grade 6 cannot be repaired by anyone.

Creation — DM Only: Grade 6 armor requires a Grade 5 armor as its foundation and a bound Level 30 soul. The binding process differs from weapon creation — the soul must accept absorption rather than impact as its fundamental orientation. This is rarer than weapon soul creation because fewer immortals at the moment of death choose reception over action. Each faction that holds a Grade 6 armor arrived at this knowledge independently. None of them have shared it. Several do not fully understand what they have.

THE GRADE 6 CONFLICT — WEAPON AND ARMOR TOGETHER

The most dangerous inventory configuration in the world.

Grade 6 weapon souls and Grade 6 armor souls instinctively hate each other. This is not a decision — it is constitutive to what they are. Weapon souls are oriented toward destruction, offense, and impact. Armor souls are oriented toward preservation, defense, and absorption. These are not different approaches to the same goal. They are philosophically opposed at the level of identity. Within the Grade 6 Soul Network they maintain a constant low-level hostility toward each other at all times regardless of proximity. When both exist in the same inventory, that hostility becomes acute. The person between them has no insulation.

The result is not mechanical damage. It is identity erosion.

Passive Degradation:

Every session a character holds both a Grade 6 weapon and Grade 6 armor, they roll a Wisdom-based Sanity Check at the start of the session. DC begins at 30 and increases by 5 every consecutive session both are maintained.

Tier accessibility is the hard truth of this check: - Standard (d20): DC 30 requires 22+ before modifier. Near-impossible. Standard tier characters effectively cannot pass — they degrade every session. - Elite (2d20): Passable at DC 30 with high Wis. DC 35+ becomes very hard. Degradation is likely but not certain early. - Legendary (2d20+d6): Genuinely passable through DC 40 with significant Wis investment. The first tier where resistance becomes real. - Immortal (3d20): Consistently passable through DC 45 at peak Wis. The only tier that can sustain both for an extended period.

Passing halts degradation for that session. Critical success recovers a small amount of Sanity. The sanity pool scales with Wisdom score.

SUBMARINE CITY — MORGAN'S BASE

DM Eyes Only. The Guild's most protected secret. Location known only to Morgan, Batman, and whoever holds all five coordinates.

Physical Specifications:

Depth: 1,000–1,500m below the Pacific Ocean surface, south of Hawaii. Below the operational depth of every known military submarine. No conventional naval asset can reach this depth intentionally, let alone accidentally. Pressure runs roughly 100–150 atmospheres — extreme by any ordinary measure, manageable with what the Guild has had centuries and multi-Earth technology to build.

Geological concealment: Built into and against a natural underwater formation on the Hawaiian-Emperor seamount chain — a seamount slope or deep canyon wall. The geological feature absorbs and scatters sonar signatures. The base reads as rock because significant portions of it are rock. It does not appear on any map because it has no reason to.

Descent time: 30–45 minutes from surface to dock. Long enough to feel like a genuine journey into somewhere else entirely. Visitors tend to arrive differently than they descended.

What It Is:

Submarine City is the Guild's operational headquarters — a permanently staffed underwater facility built across decades by people who expected to be there for a very long time. It is not a city. It is a base that has grown large enough that the word base no longer quite covers it. Corridors that go further than they should. Sections built for purposes that changed before they were finished. The feel of something lived in and expanded and adapted over generations.

Morgan has been here for most of it.

The architecture is practical necessity, what was available, and what Morgan decided was worth preserving. The feel of Rapture — scale, ambition, isolation, something that could only exist because someone with extraordinary capability decided to build it anyway, answerable to nothing. Not Rapture in the sense of decay. Submarine City is maintained. It is staffed. It is alive.

Artificial light, warm. Wide corridors. Rooms that feel like rooms. Spaces that feel like something that never got a proper name.

The Entry — Surface Level:

The Hawaii Contact: One man. Runs a fishing charter out of a small harbor on the southern coast of Hawaii's Big Island. Unremarkable boat, unremarkable demeanor. Becoming a Guild Agent — The Recruitment Chain:

Morgan does not advertise. The Guild does not post recruitment criteria. There is no application. There is no list.

To become an Agent of Morgan, a candidate must first earn the trust of an existing Agent — genuinely, over time, through demonstrated alignment of values and capability. An Agent who decides someone is ready passes them one thing: the names and current known locations of the five coordinate holders.

That is the beginning of the hunt.

The candidate must track down each holder — some cooperative, some not, some in dangerous territory, some who have moved since the intel was gathered. Each holder provides one fragment of the coordinate and frequency key. Assembling all five resolves to a location and a transmission protocol. The candidate then makes their way to Hawaii, finds the contact, and descends.

If they take the red pill, they are an Agent.

What this means for the campaign:

No faction can infiltrate the Guild by capturing a coordinate holder and extracting the information — each holder only has one piece. No single piece is useful. Infiltration requires getting close enough to a sitting Agent that they willingly pass the full holder list. The Guild has never been successfully infiltrated through this chain because the Agents who pass the chain are old enough and perceptive enough to not be fooled easily.

Jaren's path to Morgan followed this chain exactly. He was trusted by someone who trusted him enough to pass the names. He spent years collecting the pieces. His arrival at Submarine City is the completion of that process and the beginning of his full membership.

The red pill is not optional for Agents. Every Agent in the network has the microbots. Every Agent can be found. Every Agent has the kill switch inside them. Morgan does not hold this over people — but it is understood. The network's security depends on it. An Agent who is captured cannot be used as a leak indefinitely. That is the design.

THE CHIHIRO ARC — EXALTED WEAPON COMPLETION EVENT

Do not develop further until a player actively begins seeking Chihiro. All core structure is documented. Remaining open questions will resolve naturally from campaign context when the time comes.

The Setup:

Chihiro has been in seclusion for years. He knew this day would come. He has been preparing for it longer than he has been preparing the weapon.

He already has history with the Ark Society — he has made weapons for them before, lower-grade work that built a relationship and a standing promise. "Call on us if you need help." The rendezvous point has been pre-arranged. The signal was decided long before the forge was lit. When he finishes the Exalted weapon, he sends it. That is the plan. Not after — immediately. Anyone who hesitates is already behind.

The Ark Society is not surprised when the signal arrives. They have known this call was coming since they made the promise. They move.

The weapon itself — worth deciding before the arc runs. Chihiro is a pacifist and a peacemaker. His first Grade 5 creation may not be a conventional weapon. Something designed to protect rather than destroy would be entirely consistent with who he is. The most powerful crafted object of its generation built by the one person who never wanted anyone to use it to kill. This detail will shape what every faction wants it for and how they justify going after him.

The Intelligence Timeline:

Grade 5 weapons cannot stay quiet. Their creation leaves traces — Nen disturbance, unusual material signatures, the ambient energy profile of wherever they are shifting in ways intelligence networks notice.

  • Day 0: Exalted weapon completed. Signal sent to Ark Society rendezvous point.
  • Days 1–2: Traces detected by multiple intelligence networks. Anomaly flagged — not confirmed yet.
  • Days 2–3: Confirmation reaches the Great Families. Confirmation reaches the Guild. Both identify the approximate location at nearly the same time.
  • Days 3–4: Operatives mobilize from both sides.
  • Simultaneously: The Ark Society is already moving. They had a head start.

The window is narrow. Chihiro planned for exactly this window. Whether the plan holds depends on execution and on who the player character is and what they choose to do.

The Three-Way Clash:

The Great Families want Chihiro alive and working under their control. An Exalted smith independent of the Guild is a strategic advantage they cannot ignore. Operatives sent with orders to secure, not harm.

The Guild — Batman receives the intelligence. He processes it. He presents nothing outwardly. He moves.

Batman is not threatened by Chihiro. He is the opposite of threatened. He understands the power of what Chihiro knows — Grade 5 technique is extraordinarily rare knowledge and Chihiro arrived at it alone, from a different direction than Batman did. That is interesting. That is worth understanding. Batman would take precautions — careful vetting, controlled information, trust built deliberately over time — and would likely take Chihiro as a protégé. He would play it completely cool throughout. He would not show how significant he considers this moment. That is entirely consistent with who he is.

Morgan would want Chihiro protected and eventually working with the Guild. Batman arriving personally to make that happen is the right deployment.

The Ark Society is already in motion before either faction's operatives leave. Takezo, Elizabeth, Takeo, and at least one of the Triplets. They made a promise to someone who made weapons for them. They are going to keep it. They do not care about the weapon. They care about Chihiro.

The player character — Asta is the natural connection. He knows Chihiro from the Kansai Squad. He was not told about the Ark Society arrangement. Finding out that his gentle classmate has been preparing for this for years, and that the plan is already in motion, and that two of the most powerful organizations on Earth are racing toward the same point — that is the moment Asta walks into.

The Summit Fight — Three Level 30 Individuals:

Takezo / The Sakura Samurai — Level 30. Apex. The Ark Society exists because he does. Without him it falls apart — not gradually, immediately. He is the center of gravity around which every other member orbits. He came because he made a promise. He prepared for exactly this. He is equal to either opponent in raw power tier.

The Great Families Level 30 operative — sent to secure Chihiro. Equal to Takezo in power. Not here for a war — here for an acquisition. The fight is an obstacle.

The Guild Level 30 operative — most likely Batman. Cool, precise, equal to both. Here to make sure Chihiro reaches Guild protection. The fight is an inconvenience he is managing.

Three equal fighters. The fight is genuinely uncertain. Any of them could die. That uncertainty is what makes it a real fight rather than a set piece.

One of them might not walk out. If the opportunity presents itself — if the positioning is right, if someone overcommits, if the three-way dynamic creates a window — one of the three Level 30 individuals could die. The most likely candidate is the Great Families operative, but it is not guaranteed. Plan for the possibility without scripting the outcome. Let the session determine it.

The Grade 6 Opportunity:

If a Level 30 dies in this fight, their soul is present. Chihiro is the only person in the building capable of doing anything with it — and he does not know that.

Chihiro has never heard of Grade 6. He believes Grade 5 is the peak of what smithing can achieve. When he encounters the soul of a fallen Level 30, he will not recognize it as a Grade 6 opportunity. He will not know what he is looking at. He will only know that whatever this material is, it is something he has never encountered, and it does not fit any framework he has.

What happens next is not a moral calculation. It is professional curiosity. I wonder if I could. Not I wonder if I should. The same instinct that drove him into years of seclusion to master something everyone else said couldn't be done. His hands will start asking questions before his conscience has time to weigh in.

If he finds the time and the safety to try — he will try. And he will succeed.

The world will then have a thirteenth Grade 6 weapon it was not supposed to have, created by someone who did not know the ceiling existed, from curiosity rather than intention. The soul's temperament — reflecting whoever died in that fight — will define what that weapon becomes in the Grade 6 network. Chihiro will not fully understand what he made until the consequences arrive. By then it will be too late to unmake it.

Batman will understand immediately what happened. How he handles that knowledge is a character moment worth planning.

The Player's Role — The Actual Deciding Factor:

The summit fight is spectacle and obstacle. The player is the session. Racing through the chaos, managing operatives from all sides, and getting Chihiro to the rendezvous point is what determines the outcome. The three-way Level 30 fight exists to create pressure, close routes, and occasionally spill into the player's path at the worst moment.

Chihiro is not helpless. He knows where he is going. He has a plan and he is already executing it. The player isn't rescuing someone frozen — they are running alongside someone who has been preparing for this day for years and just needs cover. That distinction changes how the session feels.

The weapon is present. Whatever Chihiro created — it is there, in the chaos. Every faction knows it exists. Chihiro will not use it except as an absolute last resort. He is extremely reluctant to hand it to anyone. He built it. It cost him something real. He will not let it be used carelessly and he will not give it to someone who will treat it as a tool. If someone is dying in front of him and it is the only answer — he will hesitate even then. That hesitation is the character. Nobody walks out of this session with the weapon unless Chihiro decides they can.

If Chihiro reaches the Ark Society, the sanctuary holds — at least initially. The Ark Society honoring a pre-arranged promise under pressure from two Tier 0 organizations is their most significant public act to date. It defines how the world sees them.

The long-term answer: Chihiro eventually chooses the Guild and Batman's mentorship. Not captured. Not pressured. He chooses it because it is the only arrangement that lets him create without being owned. Batman gets a protégé. Chihiro gets protection and a peer. Morgan gets a second Exalted forge. The Great Families get nothing.

Batman would never make it feel like recruitment. He would just be present, and competent, and allow Chihiro to draw his own conclusions. That is the move.

The Great Families — Full Lore (DM Eyes Only)

What They Are

"The Great Families" is a name from their founding era — each immortal who joined brought a family, a following, a faction. Over millennia the term became irrelevant. The organization is now run purely and strictly by immortal beings. No one who isn't immortal holds meaningful authority within it.

They are the oldest continuous power structure in the universe. They predate Earth 9's nations, Earth 9's history, and most of Earth 9's civilizations. They have been doing this since Earth 1.

The Council

The Great Families are governed by a large immortal council. Major world-altering decisions require a vote. Individual members hold significant personal authority within their own operations — but anything that reshapes the trajectory of a civilization, a faction, or the Prophecy goes before the council.

This creates constant internal friction. They argue. They form alliances within the council and break them. Some hate each other. Some have respected each other for millions of years. Some don't concern themselves with personal dynamics at all. What holds it together is a shared understanding: they are all looking for the next Perfect Being, and they all know what it means to find one.

The council versus the Guild: The Guild has Morgan. Final authority rests with one person who has seen everything and carries every previous Morgan's knowledge. The Great Families have no equivalent. Their council is their greatest strength and their most persistent structural weakness — decisions take time, consensus is rare, and brilliant individual operations are sometimes vetoed by members with competing agendas.

Defection is not tolerated. If someone leaves the council they are hunted. This is not a vote. It is an understanding. Hasim and Morgan are ongoing removal problems — Hasim for centuries, Morgan for millions of years. Neither has been successfully eliminated.

Size and Reach

At any given time on Earth 9 there are approximately 10 Great Families immortals active on Earth or on the moon base. The rest are scattered across other worlds accessible through the Stargates the Great Families control. Some are dedicated to the council's goals and vote regularly. Some don't care — they don't vote and are on-call if ever needed. The latter still understand the rules about defection.

The full member list is extensive — over 20 confirmed immortals across all worlds. Individual profiles and stories to be developed when the full list is compiled.

Infrastructure — The Moon Base

The Great Families' primary coordination point is a mobile base positioned on the moon. It has engine capability but has not moved since they arrived on Earth 9. It contains a council chamber, personal quarters for immortals, and advanced large-scale teleporters that connect to similarly built receiver units distributed throughout the world. These teleporters allow rapid movement between the moon and Earth-based checkpoints without using the Stargates.

The council meets here. Major votes happen here. It is the closest thing the Great Families have to a home on Earth 9.

In addition to the moon base, numerous minor operational bases and checkpoints exist worldwide — used by operatives and immortals conducting field work. Makima maintains a major personal base somewhere in America — location and details to be established.

Universal Objectives

Every Great Families immortal on Earth 9 operates with two overarching goals that color everything they do individually:

Finding the Great Gate before the Syzygy: The gate is hidden until the universe summons the prophesied people to it. The Great Families want to locate it early — to be positioned and ready to ensure permanent activation when the moment arrives. Identifying the prophesied individuals before they are summoned is an active intelligence priority.

Identifying the next Perfect Being: Anyone on Earth 9 approaching Legendary tier gets watched. Anyone approaching Immortal tier gets active surveillance and council discussion. Founders have a sharper instinct for recognizing the potential — they knew the original and carry that reference point. Most candidates are eliminated quickly. A few are being monitored very closely right now.

How the Progression Works

Every million years a syzygy of all planets in the solar system causes the universe's Nen to temporarily expand. This powers the Stargates.

During the Syzygy on Earth 9:

  1. All basic gates are forced open simultaneously for 3–4 hours. Whatever is on the other side of every gate comes through. This is the primary mechanism of the apocalyptic destruction that follows every Syzygy — not just the Nen expansion, but every gate opening at once. Earth 4's Nen creatures through the Knights' gate. Earth 5's machine entities through Russia's gate. Earth 3's angels and demons through the Great Families' gate. Earth 8's alien ecosystem through the Preserve's gate. All of it, simultaneously, for 3–4 hours.

  2. One specific basic gate on Earth 9 temporarily connects to Earth 10's Great Gate. Two Great Gates cannot connect to each other — so the choosing of the next world routes through a basic gate instead. This is the mechanism by which Earth 10 is chosen as the next planet to be marked and awakened. Which basic gate carries this connection is unknown. Finding it before the Syzygy arrives is a live intelligence priority for every faction that knows the truth.

  3. Earth 9's own Great Gate opens. The universe summons the prophesied people to it. What happens at the Great Gate — who is there, what they choose — determines whether the activation becomes permanent or the window closes after 3–4 hours.

If activation is permanent: All gates stay open. The Great Families get their network. Earth 9 and Earth 10 and every connected world become linked indefinitely.

If activation is temporary: The window closes. The gates seal. The destruction was for nothing and everything resets for another million years on Earth 10.

The Great Families move the moon base through space to Earth 10's solar system after the Syzygy. The cycle continues.

The Great Cataclysm — The Founding Event:

Before the Great Families existed, before the Stargate network, before any of the structures currently visible in this quadrant were built — something came from outside it. That is why they cannot be cleanly called villains. That is also why they cannot be trusted.

THE LARGER UNIVERSE (Far Future — Do Not Develop Yet)

Everything established in this campaign — the Stargate network, the Syzygy cycle, the Great Families, the Guild, all nine Earths, all current factions — exists within one quadrant of the universe.

Three other quadrants exist. They have not been reached by the Stargate network. They contain their own civilizations, their own threats, and their own forms of power. Some are savage. Some are organized. What they represent to this quadrant is an eventual existential threat — not imminent, but certain on a long enough timeline.

WHO KNOWS

Only the founding members of the Great Families who remained committed to the cause know about the other quadrants and what they contain. This knowledge is sealed. It has never been shared outside that circle.

Hasim does not know. He was not a founding member — he was the first person to reach Level 30 and be recruited after the Great Families had already formed. He was brought in for what he was, not as a co-founder. The innermost secret was never meant for him. He was useful. He was not their equal in the way the original four were. When he eventually saw enough to leave, he left with a complete understanding of everything they had shown him and no knowledge of everything they had not. He has spent his entire existence fighting against something he fundamentally misunderstood. He never will know. The Great Families let him walk away because killing a founding-era recruit of his capability creates more chaos than managing him from a distance — and because he can never expose what he was never told.

Morgan Le Fay knows. She was present at the founding and is one of the two who carry this. She has been conscious in stasis on Earth 1 this entire time. She knows what is coming. She has been unable to act. She has watched the Guild — the organization founded in response to the Great Families — fight to dismantle the only preparation that exists for a threat the Guild doesn't know about.

The current Morgan does not know. The Guild has been operating its entire existence without this context. Every decision they have made — including Earth 7 — was made in the dark.

When Le Fay is rescued, current Morgan receives not just the trade secret but the full picture. Everything changes. The Guild's purpose, the Great Families' purpose, the question of whether opposing them was ever fully right — all of it becomes genuinely complicated in a way it wasn't before.

Hasim must never learn this. Not because knowing would break him — he might survive knowing. But because what he would do with it is unpredictable in a way nothing else in the campaign is. A man who built his entire existence on opposing the Great Families, who has committed every cruelty in service of that opposition, who left before he could be told the reason — if he learns the reason now, after everything, that is not a character who processes information cleanly. The Great Families understand this. The secret stays sealed partly for this reason.

THE GREAT FAMILIES' TRUE POSITION

They feel their ambition and their strength as necessity. Most of them also genuinely enjoy absolute power and domination — that enjoyment is real, it has grown over millions of years of unchecked authority, and it corrupts the necessity underneath without replacing it. Both things are true simultaneously. They are preparing for something real. They have also become something monstrous in the process of preparing for it.

The search for Level 31 is not conquest for its own sake. It is the construction of a weapon. The Perfect Being was the intended answer to what they know is coming from other quadrants. Morgan killed him before the Syzygy believing she was stopping something terrible. She was. She may have also destroyed the only thing that could have stopped something worse.

This is the deepest expression of the campaign's thesis. People are as much the problem as they are the solution. Morgan's greatest act and her greatest mistake may be the same act. The Great Families' necessity and their cruelty are inseparable. The solution to one thing became the architecture of another problem. None of them are wrong. None of them are right. They have all been operating with incomplete information for millions of years.

The Facetakers (Primordial Parasitic Entities — DM Eyes Only)

Players should never encounter the word "Facetaker." They will encounter NPCs behaving strangely, communities decaying, and — eventually — something that should not exist. The name belongs to Hunters only.

Origin

The Facetakers are primordial. They were not created — they were born, in the same moment and from the same event that produced the universe itself. Where the expanding universe produces light, matter, and life, the darkness outside its boundary produces something else. The Facetakers exist in that outer dark as a twisted mirrored version of humanity — shapes that resemble people only approximately, born from nothing but instinct and hunger.

Their origin is unknown in-world. The Ancients — the only civilization to ever study them seriously — documented what they observed but could not explain what the Facetakers are at a fundamental level. That documentation is sealed on Earth 20. The Hunters have been operating without it for as long as the Hunters have existed.

This is intentional. The mystery is part of the horror. Do not resolve it.

Nature & Taxonomy

All Facetakers belong to the same primordial species. They share origin, instinct, and feeding mechanism. Beyond that they are radically individual — each one a distinct biological horror shaped by whatever dark form it developed in the void outside the universe.

They have no intelligence. They do not plan, strategize, communicate, or form relationships. They operate entirely on instinct. The behavior that looks like cunning — the careful selection of a host, the slow patient feeding, the decision to move on before discovery — is not thought. It is hunger with very old reflexes.

They do not communicate with each other. Two Facetakers in the same city will recognize each other by instinct and steer clear. There is no coordination, no territory negotiation, no acknowledgment beyond mutual avoidance. They are not a faction in any organizational sense — they are a phenomenon.

Appearance reflects nature. A Facetaker built from shadow and darkness will embody those mechanics in combat and drive its host toward behaviors connected to concealment and predation. A Facetaker built from teeth and bone will manifest accordingly. The connection between revealed form, host behavior, and corruption field is always thematically coherent — they are all expressions of the same underlying creature.

⚑ DM Note: When designing individual Facetakers, work outward from the revealed form. What does it look like? That determines how its host behaves, what its corruption field does to surrounding NPCs, and what mechanics appear in the boss encounter.

The Host

The original person is dead. The moment the Facetaker completes transfer, the host is gone — not suppressed, not suffering somewhere inside, simply no longer present. The body continues. The life continues. No Hunter has ever found a method to reverse this. If one is ever discovered it should be treated as a campaign-level revelation.

The host body is maintained and operated with eerie competence. Facetakers are extraordinarily good at passing as human — the right expressions, the expected words, the correct behaviors. This is not intelligence. It is mimicry, honed across centuries of instinct-driven observation.

Detection is possible but requires expertise. For most NPCs nothing will ever seem wrong. For attentive characters, small things may surface — a pause before responding that runs a beat too long, a reaction that doesn't track to the stimulus, an absence of involuntary physical responses. For a Hunter, these tells are a language learned over years.

⚑ DM Note: Detection skill checks — Sense Motive, Awareness, Nature. DC scales with the Facetaker's level and how long it has been in the current host. Hunters receive significant bonuses from training and the Brand.

The host appears completely normal unless someone with expertise knows how to probe. The only visible evidence of the creature's presence exists in the people around the host — not the host itself.

The Corruption Field

Every Facetaker radiates a passive aura simply by existing. This is not a combat mechanic — it is environmental and social, the creature's presence made toxic to the world around it over time.

Mechanism: The aura affects NPCs in sustained contact with the host — coworkers, family members, neighbors, close friends. It does not work through casual proximity. It requires repeated exposure. High-level characters (roughly Level 10+) are largely resistant.

Effect depends on the creature. The specific flavor of the corruption always reflects the Facetaker's nature and appearance. Shadow types breed paranoia and isolation. Biological horror types push toward violence and animalistic behavior. Collection-based types erode their targets toward obsession, self-harm, and hoarding. Emotional siphon types strip away joy and meaning until nothing remains.

Affected NPCs will not know something is wrong. They will experience these shifts as natural — as simply who they have become.

This is how Hunters locate Facetakers before direct investigation. Clusters of suicides. Unusual rates of violent incidents in a neighborhood. A family where three members died of unrelated causes within five years. Hunters learn to read these patterns the way a doctor reads symptoms.

The Feeding Cycle

The Facetaker feeds on life — the ambient energy of living people in proximity to the host, siphoned passively over years. The corruption field is a byproduct of this siphoning, not a deliberate act.

Duration: 2–10 years per host, most commonly 4–8.

Decay pattern: People close to the host begin dying first, from causes that look completely natural. Suicide. Cancer. A car accident. Heart failure in someone too young. None of these deaths examined individually would raise suspicion. The pattern only becomes visible to someone looking for it.

Triggers to move on: - Feeding ground exhausted — close contacts have died or drifted away - Host body terminally ill or aging past utility - A genuine threat enters the host's regular life (Level 30 entity as a frequent contact; a Hunter actively investigating) - The creature detects it is being hunted — it has an internal alarm for this, developed over centuries of evolutionary pressure

⚑ DM Note: The flee response is instinctual, not strategic. The Facetaker does not make a plan. It simply feels the threat and begins transfer. A Hunter who moves too slowly may arrive to find the old host dead and the creature already hidden in someone new.

The Transfer Process

The only window where a Facetaker is fully vulnerable without being an immediate threat.

Conditions required: The new host must be asleep. Complete darkness — the process cannot proceed in light. 5–10 minutes uninterrupted.

What it looks like in the dark: The Facetaker sheds its current host, which collapses to the ground dead. The creature becomes briefly visible — its true form exposed between hosts. Tendrils or some form of organic connection extend from the dead host, through the creature, and into the sleeping new host. All three are physically linked for the duration.

Bringing light during transfer reveals all of this instantly. The creature cannot complete transfer in light. It will be forced into the open — revealed form, combat begins immediately.

If the Facetaker is killed during transfer: The creature dies. The old host is already dead. The new host survives — they were only asleep, the creature never fully entered. They wake up with no knowledge of what almost happened to them.

If the creature completes transfer uninterrupted: The new host stands up. The old host is dead. The creature is hidden again. The hunt resets.

Finding the transfer window is not just the safest kill — it is the only kill that leaves a living survivor behind.

The Revealed Form

When a Facetaker is forced into the open — host killed, transfer interrupted, cornered — it emerges immediately and becomes dangerous without delay.

The host body is nothing. What remains is an ordinary corpse with no unusual properties.

The environment warps, but does not disappear. The physical space remains intact. The creature augments it — imposing its own nature onto the surfaces and space around it. What this looks like is entirely creature-specific.

The Facetaker dissolves completely when killed. No remains. No physical evidence. No loot. No body to study. Whatever it was simply ceases to exist. The only record of a hunt is the Hunter's memory of it.

⚑ DM Note: Every piece of knowledge the Hunters carry came from observing living Facetakers — never from examining dead ones. The only things they know are things Hunters survived long enough to remember.

Boss structure: Each Facetaker is a Level 20–30 encounter designed around the individual creature. Most spawn adds. Most alter the play space. Each one is a unique set piece.

Faction Dynamics

Recognition between Facetakers: Pure instinct. No processing required. One simply knows another on sight. This cannot be suppressed or overridden.

Detection methods available to others:

Sense Motive / behavioral reads Hunters, high-WIS characters
Awareness + Nature Characters attuned to biology and wrong patterns
Nen perception Nen users — the aura reads as deeply and specifically wrong

Territorial behavior: Feeding grounds are social — defined by the host's personal network rather than geography. Two Facetakers in the same city will avoid each other's contacts. If their networks overlap, one will typically begin preparing to move. No confrontation. They simply want nothing to do with each other.

Hunter recognition: Facetakers have an instinctive alarm for Hunters specifically. They cannot articulate why certain individuals feel dangerous. They simply do. An experienced Hunter entering a Facetaker's life will eventually trigger this alarm — which is why Hunters study from a distance before getting close.

The Hunters (Oldest Living Faction — DM Eyes Only)

The Hunters predate every other named faction in the campaign. The Great Families are old. The Guild is ancient. The Hunters were there at the beginning, created in direct response to a threat civilization has never officially acknowledged.

Origin

The Hunters were founded during the age of the Ancients — the Level 31 civilization that controlled all 20 worlds before the unknown catastrophe that sealed Earth 20. When the Ancients discovered the Facetakers, they also created the institutional response. That response has outlasted the civilization that built it by an unknown number of centuries.

All Hunter knowledge is oral. There are no written records, no archive, no codex. Every piece of knowledge the Hunters carry exists only in the memory of living Hunters, passed by voice to the next generation. This was a deliberate choice by the founding Hunters, for security reasons no longer held in anyone's memory.

The original Hunter headquarters was on Earth 20. Lost when the Ancients fell and the world was sealed. Finding it would mean finding the only written record of everything the Hunters were ever taught and everything the Ancients knew about the Facetakers.

⚑ DM Note: Earth 20 access requires Level 31. Late-campaign material. Flag and leave.

Numbers & Standing

Fewer than 25 active Hunters exist at any time across all worlds. Whether this is a deliberate ceiling or simply the emergent result of how difficult the work is — likely both — it has held for as long as anyone in the faction can recall.

Official Hunters receive a universal pardon. Every faction with knowledge of the Facetakers extends standing to Hunters. A Hunter can move through any informed faction's territory without being treated as an enemy. They are left alone to do their work.

Hunters work alone. The time spent alongside another Hunter is almost exclusively the training period. After a student becomes official, both go their separate ways. Team-ups happen but they are the exception.

Becoming a Hunter

The process cannot be initiated by the student. A person can only make themselves findable — through reputation, visible skill, being in the right place. If a Hunter notices them and believes in them, contact will be made.

Stage 1 — The Test: The Hunter designs and administers a personal evaluation covering all six attributes and a wide range of skills. This is not purely a combat test. Perception, patience, deduction, improvisation under pressure, and genuine attention toward what is wrong in a room are weighted as heavily as fighting ability. Passing makes someone a Prospect, not a student.

Stage 2 — The Hunts: The Hunter takes the Prospect on a series of hunts — observing, assisting, gradually taking more active roles. The Hunter watches for how the person behaves when they see the revealed form for the first time. How they handle a hunt that ended badly. Whether they study or only react. Most Prospects do not complete this stage. Some quit. Some die. A few are dismissed.

Stage 3 — Full Studenthood: If the Hunter decides the Prospect has what is needed, they formally take them on as a student. No ceremony. Training is measured in years. The Hunter passes the full oral tradition and evaluates continuously. The goal is the moment the student no longer needs them.

Stage 4 — The Branding: When the Hunter judges a student ready — their call entirely — the official initiation takes place.

The Brand

The central ritual of official initiation is the application of a permanent brand: a symbol drawn from the Ancients' original vocabulary of Facetaker study, one of several cursed marks used to interact with and identify the entities.

Appearance: The brand predates any known written human language. It reads as an abstract design to those who don't know what it is. To another Hunter it is immediately recognizable as genuine. Applied by the senior Hunter using a method passed down through their own initiation. Painful. Permanent.

What it does:

Aura resistance. Complete immunity to the Facetaker's passive corruption field. A branded Hunter can spend years in a Facetaker's social environment without being eroded toward violence, depression, or suicidal ideation. Without this, sustained investigation would destroy the investigator before the work was finished.

Proximity alarm. In the presence of an active Facetaker, the brand becomes physically uncomfortable — a heat, a tension in the skin, a sensation that is not quite pain. It sharpens as the Hunter gets closer. Not a precise locator, but confirmation of what other observations are already suggesting. Cannot be suppressed.

Faction identification. The brand cannot be faked. A Hunter can identify another Hunter's brand on sight and know it as real.

⚑ DM Note: The brand mechanics above are proposed — confirm or adjust as needed before using them at the table.
